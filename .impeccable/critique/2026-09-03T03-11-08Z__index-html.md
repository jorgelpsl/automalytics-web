## Design Specificity Verdict

**Moderately authored, with visible seams of reuse — the specificity lives in the copy and two case studies, not in the design system.**

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** The strongest evidence of real authorship is "Casos Reales": the two demo sites (dental clinic, veterinary) have genuinely distinct visual identities and localized copy (CLP pricing, "Fonasa e Isapres," Chile-specific details) — not template filler. But the chrome around that content is a generic dark-mode SaaS shell, and the source code admits it: a literal comment at the top of the stylesheet reads *"Repurposed to match Prospectos CRM's dark/lime palette... all three now resolve to the lime accent family instead of their original hues."* `--cyan` and `--purple` now resolve to the identical value, and `--purple` is never referenced anywhere. An unrelated SaaS product could lift this exact navbar, particle canvas, and floating-WhatsApp system unchanged.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B), independently corroborating the above:** The CLI detector flagged `gradient-text` at two locations — `.stat-num` and `.about-mini .num`. Assessment B, working in total isolation from Assessment A and using a completely different method (grep across the whole file + the live browser DOM scan), found **the exact same CSS is dead code**: zero HTML elements anywhere in the document use those classes, and the live browser detector confirms zero live occurrences. Two independent methods converged on the same conclusion from different angles — this is a genuinely reliable finding, not a fluke of either method. The browser pass additionally found, live on the rendered page: 8 instances of bounce-easing (`.service-icon` hover, `cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.3,1.4)` — the `1.4` overshoots 1.0), 7 repeated "kicker" labels above section headings, 2 zero-offset colored glow shadows on the WhatsApp CTA button, an infinitely-pulsing hero badge dot, and an image that renders at `opacity:0` until scrolled into view.

**Visual overlays:** Script injection into the live page succeeded and the detector ran correctly (confirmed via a real DOM mutation test), but this session has no persistent visible browser tab to show you directly — it's a headless sandbox with no GUI surfaced to you. The overlay's findings are folded into this report instead of a `[Human]` tab; no visible-to-you overlay is available this run.

## Overall Impression

The page's actual conversion mechanics (the live lead form, the two differentiated case studies, dark/light theme, CLP-localized pricing) are more real and more finished than the surface-level "AI slop" checklist suggests — this isn't a templated throwaway. But the specific palette is a literal reskin of a sibling internal product (the CRM), roughly a third of the styled components never render because the markup for them was cut and the CSS wasn't, and the page's two highest-stakes moments — the hero ask and the pricing decision — are also its two most cognitively overloaded. The single biggest opportunity is closing the gap between "styled but unused" (a stats/trust row already built, sitting dormant) and "asked for too soon" (the lead form, at the very top, before any trust has been earned).

## What's Working

1. **The hero lead form is functionally real**, not a mockup — a live POST to `/api/lead` with honeypot spam protection and genuine success/failure states, not a "contact us" link buried below the fold like most competing agency sites.
2. **The two case-study demos are genuinely differentiated**, not palette-swapped clones — concrete, clickable proof behind the "100% a tu medida" claim.
3. **Color contrast is solid and verified in both themes**: body text measures 5.79:1 (dark) and 5.77:1 (light) against their backgrounds — both comfortably clear WCAG AA on a saturated lime-on-black palette, which isn't a given.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The nav's primary "Cotizar" CTA is completely off-screen and unclickable between ~769–975px viewport width**
- Why it matters: measured directly — at 900px width, `.nav-cta` renders at x:904–993px while the viewport is only 900px wide, silently swallowed by `overflow-x:hidden`. This band covers common small-laptop and split-screen widths, not an edge case.
- Fix: drop the mobile-nav breakpoint to cover this range, or make the nav-links pill actually shrink before `.nav-right` gets pushed off.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] Below-the-fold content depends entirely on JavaScript firing scroll events — with JS disabled, the page is nearly empty**
- Why it matters: verified directly — every `[data-aos]` element sits at `opacity:0` by default (line 723), and only an `IntersectionObserver` in inline JS ever adds the class that reveals it. That's every service card, pricing card, FAQ item, and both case studies — the entire value proposition and all pricing. This directly undercuts the site's own SEO investment (full OG tags, JSON-LD schema) for any crawler that doesn't simulate real scroll motion.
- Fix: add a `<noscript>` override forcing `[data-aos]{opacity:1!important;transform:none!important}`, or stop gating first-render content on JS at all for a marketing page.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] Cognitive overload concentrated at the two highest-stakes moments: the hero and pricing**
- Why it matters: 4 of 8 cognitive-load checklist items fail, and they cluster exactly where conversion happens. The hero shows a live 4-field form *and* two separate CTA buttons simultaneously (no single focus). Pricing shows 6 simultaneous SKUs (3 website tiers + 3 automation tiers) with split upfront/monthly fees and no combined total shown anywhere — the visitor must do that math themselves.
- Fix: in the hero, make the chat widget the singular action and demote "Ver Planes" to a plain text link. On each pricing card, show a combined first-year total next to the split fees.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

**[P2] The floating lead-capture card ignores `prefers-reduced-motion` — the one animation that should never be exempt**
- Why it matters: the reduced-motion block correctly disables the mascot, card-glow, and other transitions, but `.hero-bot-card`'s continuous `float 4s infinite` animation is not in that list — this is the actual lead form, drifting ±14px for a visitor who explicitly asked for reduced motion. It also made the send button an unstable click target in automated testing (Playwright reported "element is not stable"), suggesting real misclicks are plausible too.
- Fix: add `.hero-bot-card { animation: none !important; }` to the existing reduced-motion block.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P2] No quantified trust signal anywhere, despite the CSS for one already being built and confirmed dead by both assessments**
- Why it matters: `.stats-bar`/`.stat-num`/`.about-mini .num` are fully styled but never appear in the HTML — confirmed independently by both the design review (manual grep) and the detector (source scan + live DOM scan finding zero occurrences). There's no visible number anywhere (sites delivered, years operating) and no testimonial. Combined with the non-refundable-setup-fee term being disclosed only inside one optional FAQ answer rather than next to the price, a visitor sizing up a $150,000–$650,000 CLP commitment gets no quantified reassurance at the moment they need it most.
- Fix: populate the already-built stats row with real numbers; surface the non-refundable term directly on the pricing cards.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Icon-only mobile menu close button has no accessible name**
- Why it matters: `#mobileClose` renders only "✕" with no `aria-label`, inconsistent with the rest of the codebase — the theme toggle and scroll-to-top button both correctly label their icon-only controls. A screen reader user gets no meaningful name for this control.
- Fix: add `aria-label="Cerrar menú"` to match the existing pattern.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`
